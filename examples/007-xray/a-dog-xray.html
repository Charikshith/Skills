How to read it

  1. Skull and jawThe skull protects the brain. The long muzzle holds the nose and teeth, including the big canine tooth. The lower jaw, the mandible, is the only part of the skull that moves.
  2. The spineSeven neck, thirteen chest and seven lower-back vertebrae carry the body. The sacrum joins the hips and the tail vertebrae follow. Inside the spine runs the spinal cord with the signals from the brain.
  3. Rib cageThirteen pairs of ribs arch from the chest vertebrae down to the breastbone. They form a cage round the heart and lungs. The last ribs are short and float free.
  4. Front legsA dog has no real collar bone, so muscle holds the shoulder blade to the ribs. Then come the humerus, the radius and ulna (the elbow points back), the wrist bones and the paw bones. A dog stands on its toes.
  5. Hips and hind legsThe pelvis joins the spine to the hind legs. The femur, the kneecap, the tibia and fibula and the paw bones push the dog forward. The joint that points back is the hock, the ankle.

Key takeaways

Same bones, different shape

A dog has nearly the same bone groups as you: skull, spine, ribs, arms and legs. They are just arranged to run on four legs.

The knee is not where it looks

The joint that bends backwards in a hind leg is the ankle (hock). The real knee, the stifle, is higher and bends forward, like yours.

Built for speed and spring

No rigid collar bone lets the shoulders swing freely, and a flexible spine adds to the stride.

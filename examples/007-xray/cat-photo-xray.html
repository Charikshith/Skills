How to read it

  1. Skull and jawA cat's skull is short and round, with big eye sockets and long canine teeth. The lower jaw, the mandible, hangs below it.
  2. The arched spineSeven neck, thirteen chest and seven lower-back vertebrae form the curve that lets a cat curl into a ball. The tail vertebrae wrap round the body. The spinal cord runs inside.
  3. Rib cageThirteen pairs of ribs arch from the chest vertebrae towards the breastbone. They protect the heart and lungs, and they curl up with the spine.
  4. Front legsA cat has only a tiny collar bone, so muscles hold the shoulder blade to the ribs. This lets the front legs reach far forward. Here they lie folded under the chin.
  5. Hips and hind legsThe pelvis joins the spine to the hind legs. Folded up, the femur and the tibia lie side by side with the long foot bones.

Key takeaways

Flexible by design

A cat's backbone has about 50 small bones with soft discs between them. That is why it can curl up so tightly.

An illustration, not a scan

The photo is filtered to look like an x-ray, and the skeleton is a drawing placed by eye on the cat. It is not a real x-ray, and not made by AI.

Walks on its toes

Like a dog, a cat stands on its toes. The part of the hind leg that points backwards is the ankle, the hock.

How to read it

  1. Blood comes homeTwo big veins, the vena cavae, bring oxygen-poor blood back from the body: one from the head and arms, one from the lower body. Both empty into the right atrium.
  2. Right atriumThe right atrium collects the blood and pushes it down through the tricuspid valve, a one-way door with three flaps, into the right ventricle.
  3. Right ventricleThe right ventricle squeezes and sends the blood up through the pulmonary valve into the pulmonary trunk.
  4. The lungsThe pulmonary artery splits to the left and right lung. There the blood drops off carbon dioxide and picks up oxygen. It is the one artery that carries oxygen-poor blood.
  5. Left atriumPulmonary veins bring the oxygen-rich blood back to the left atrium. It flows through the mitral valve, which has two flaps, into the left ventricle.
  6. Left ventricleThe left ventricle has the thickest wall because it must push blood round the whole body. It pumps through the aortic valve into the aorta.
  7. The aortaThe aorta arches over the heart and runs down the body, branching to the brain, organs and muscles. The used blood returns to the vena cavae and the loop starts again.

Key takeaways

Two pumps in one

The right side sends blood to the lungs, the left side sends it to the body. The blood passes through the heart twice on each full round.

Left and right are mirrored

In a front view, the heart's left side is on the right of the picture, as if you faced the person. The left ventricle forms the tip that points down and left.

Valves keep it one-way

The "lub-dub" you hear is the valves closing: first the tricuspid and mitral, then the pulmonary and aortic.
